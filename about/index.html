Ontology and knowledge graph architecture

My core technical focus is knowledge representation. In OCOR (Operational Causal Ontology Runtime), my current project, the ontology is written as code and compiled into a versioned, content-addressed canonical model. Agents reach data only through typed, policy-filtered named queries and tools, so the ontology works as a governance layer: it defines which data, queries and actions are allowed.

At OCOR’s W3C boundary, RDF and JSON-LD publication, SHACL Core validation and SPARQL are prototyped on Apache Jena / Fuseki. Its provenance model, which extends W3C PROV-O, is designed.

Selected evidence

Implementation in development

OCOR

A vendor-neutral semantic core that connects ontology definitions, evidence, governed decisions and controlled actions. I designed its ontology-as-code metamodel. I built its ontology compiler, which turns each ontology version into a deterministic, content-addressed canonical model with a semantic diff and migration checks; a named-query gateway that checks policy before any read; governed agent memory with hybrid retrieval and consolidation that keeps conflicting claims apart; a causal scenario runtime that abstains when an effect is not identifiable; and an agent kernel with typed tools, budgets and a kill switch.

Limitation: an implementation in development on a wholly synthetic scenario, not a production system. Qualification is in progress.

Research direction: Neuromorphic Inference

“Neuromorphic Inference” is the name of the lab and of a research direction: brain-inspired principles for how AI systems activate, connect and update structured knowledge, starting with context-dependent activation and relevance propagation over knowledge graphs.

Scope: software research, with no spiking neural networks and no neuromorphic hardware. These mechanisms are a research direction, not implemented capabilities; OCOR builds the components they would act on.

Psychology-informed multi-agent systems

My academic training in Psychological Sciences gives me an engineering perspective on authority bias, conformity, groupthink, social loafing and overconfidence. I do not treat language models as humans, nor do I assume they have human intentions. Instead, I translate these dynamics into concrete failure risks, structural safeguards and testing strategies.

The designs I work on separate procedural control from reasoning authority, embed structured dissent and falsifiable objections, use blind reviews and external checks, test agent ablations, and compare multi-agent setups against single-agent baselines with equal compute. The evaluation designs cover accuracy, resistance to misleading prompts, error propagation, traceability and cost.

Scope: these are conceptual and experimental designs. They are not a validated framework or completed benchmark.

Research-led engineering method

Method

Core Question → Literature Review → Stated Assumptions → Success & Failure Criteria → Instrumentation → Validation.

I separate evidence, inference and hypothesis, and I set evaluation criteria before I claim results. I instrument systems to monitor quality, cost, latency and failure rates, and I revise architectural choices against observed results and known limitations.
