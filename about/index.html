Psychology-informed multi-agent systems

Formal training in Psychological Sciences informs a non-anthropomorphic engineering lens on authority bias, conformity, groupthink, social loafing and confidence dominance. I do not treat language models as people or assume that they possess human motives. I translate these concepts into observable failure hypotheses, structural controls and evaluation designs.

Proposed interests include separating procedural from epistemic authority; structured dissent and falsifiable objections; blind review and external verification; role permutation and agent ablation; adversarial-leader testing; and compute-equivalent single-agent baselines. Measurement targets task correctness, dissent robustness, cascading error, traceability and cost.

Scope: these are conceptual and experimental designs under development. They are not a validated framework or completed benchmark.

Research-led engineering method

Method

Question → literature review → explicit assumptions → success and failure criteria → instrumentation → validation.

I review recent academic and technical literature, separate evidence, inference and hypothesis, and define evaluation criteria before claiming outcomes. I instrument systems to collect quality, cost, latency and failure evidence, then revise architectural decisions against observed results and documented limitations.
